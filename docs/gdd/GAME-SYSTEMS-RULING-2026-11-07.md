# Game systems ruling, Josh, 2026-11-07 (restated by the Claude judge lane; Josh corrects, the file changes)

Supersedes the "nothing changes stats" wording in the wardrobe rules, the NEEDs Shop v1.1 design and PR #86. Pay-to-win stays never. Josh's definition of never pay-to-win is below, and it is the rule every item follows.

## 1. The stat rule
- Items carry stats. Every item in a tier gives the same percentage; the player's choice is which stat, never how much. Any hat is 10 percent experience. The fish costume is 10 percent fishing experience. The cooking apron and the chef's hat are 10 percent cooking experience. Weapons and pets follow the same pattern: same percentages, class-specific reasons to choose one over another.
- Everything sold for cash can also be earned or bought in game. The in-game price is high enough that the cash version is a convenience of time, never an advantage of power. "Convenience" is the only thing the NEEDs shop sells; the WANTs come from the stats on everything.
- Every skin fits any body (creator rule): the apron and the chef's hat are both for any body.

## 2. Pets
- Pets give 5, 10 or 15 percent by pet level, in the stat their kind is for (the hawk: attack speed, the archer's pick). Class-specific reasons, same percentages.
- A pet that is out needs feeding every 30 to 60 minutes; a missed feed starts a starvation timer. Two deaths can be revived in game, each revive costing more than the last because the pet has leveled. The third death is animal cruelty: only a cash revive brings it back.
- Auto-feed is sold as a convenience (not feeding on a timer), never as a boost.

## 3. Convenience items that sell
- Skill-tree resets: in game at a high price, or a cash convenience item.
- Fable's Anvil: a portable pop-up campsite (tow-behind triangle tent shape) for repairs and potions without walking to a shop; uses capped at 3 to 5 by level and by the size of the HP, mana or energy pool.
- Buff scrolls, solo and party, timed: sold in the shop and craftable in game from recipes.
- The Gemini HUD: goggles or glasses; Select or Esc opens the HUD; talk-to-text through the player's own free Gemini sign-in (their own email auth, built in one day) to browse, send mail, and search our forum for the recipe of a food or elixir. First-person play is allowed for those who want it.

## 4. Classes and builds
- Monk: energy only; HP and mana sit at 1; Focus is the awakening; before awakening, normal HP and mana potions. Energy use after awakening is our own take on action-game resource systems, built unique.
- Human defender: the vitality path (defender awakening). Knight: the damage, melee path. Dragon characters: HP-based surprise builds found later.
- Priest or healer: a full reset to INT can make a mace-and-shield melee build; a wizard can go warlock. The templar name is not used; the awakening is a choice. Adult-humor class names stay out of the game.
- Every class can cast; a dexterity ranger can skill into magic for a hybrid build that levels easier or beats a particular Captain.

## 5. Captains
- Each area's mob spawns have a mini-boss, the Captain, on a rotation set by the week. Not the 22-hour world boss and not its drop rate.
- Captains drop second-tier weapon and armor materials that Fable's forge needs to forge or enhance. Buyable in the shop, farmable in game. Some Captains are weak to magic, some strong against it, so builds matter.
- The Captains feed the NEEDs. Design later; the ruling is recorded now.
