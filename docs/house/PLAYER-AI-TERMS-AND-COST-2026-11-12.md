### Labeled Assumptions for the Arithmetic

1. **Assumption A1 (95% / 5% Routing Split):** Exactly 95 percent of a player's daily exchanges are answered by Haiku and 5 percent are routed to Opus:
   - **Light (20 exchanges):** 19 Haiku exchanges and 1 Opus exchange.
   - **Normal (60 exchanges):** 57 Haiku exchanges and 3 Opus exchanges.
   - **Heavy (200 exchanges):** 190 Haiku exchanges and 10 Opus exchanges.
2. **Assumption A2 (Cached Prefix Size = 2,000 Tokens on Claude 5.5; 4,096 Tokens on Claude 4.5/4.6):** The static prompt prefix (world lore, NPC persona, child-safety system prompt, and sensitive-topic playbook) is **2,000 tokens** on Claude 5.5 (exceeding the 512-token minimum cacheable length on Haiku 5.5 and Opus 5.5). For the secondary comparison on Claude Haiku 4.5 and Opus 4.6, the prefix is padded to **4,096 tokens** because 4,096 tokens is the hard minimum cacheable prompt length for both models (`https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching`, read 2026-11-12).
3. **Assumption A3 (Uncached Dynamic Input = 200 Tokens per Exchange):** Each turn sends **200 uncached input tokens** covering the player's short message (~50 tokens) plus live game state and recent turn delta (~150 tokens).
4. **Assumption A4 (Output Length = 100 Tokens on Haiku; 200 Tokens on Opus):** Everyday NPC replies on Haiku average **100 output tokens** (~75 words). Sensitive-topic responses on Opus average **200 output tokens** (~150 words) so the NPC can answer thoughtfully and provide a real human or helpline resource.
5. **Assumption A5 (5-Minute Cache Write vs. Cache Hit Counts per Day):** Because each cache hit refreshes the 5-minute TTL at no extra charge (`https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching`, read 2026-11-12), a player writes the Haiku cache once per play session: **2 Haiku cache writes** in Light (17 hits), **3 Haiku cache writes** in Normal (54 hits), and **5 Haiku cache writes** in Heavy (185 hits). Because Opus is invoked rarely (only 5 percent of turns) and caches are isolated per model, Opus has a higher cache-write ratio: **1 write / 0 hits** in Light (1 call total), **2 writes / 1 hit** in Normal (3 calls total), and **4 writes / 6 hits** in Heavy (10 calls total).
6. **Assumption A6 (Newer Tokenizer Note on Claude 4.7+ and 5.5):** Anthropic notes on `https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing` (read 2026-11-12) that Claude 4.7 and later models (including 5.5) use a newer tokenizer that produces approximately 30 percent more tokens for the same English text than Claude 4.6 and earlier. ## 5. Children's Privacy: What COPPA Requires of a Free Game That Under-13 Players Can Reach When It Stores Chat and Memory

**Verdict:** **Being free to play does not exempt DREAM Online from COPPA. Under the FTC's COPPA Rule (`16 CFR Part 312`, read 2026-11-12), storing free-text chat, cross-session AI memory, persistent user identifiers (beyond basic internal operations), or voice audio from any child under 13 is legally the collection of "personal information." Before collecting or storing chat or AI memory for an under-13 player, the game must use a neutral age gate, send a Direct Notice to the child's parent, and obtain Verifiable Parental Consent (VPC) — while allowing under-13 children who do not have parental consent to play the game using safe pre-scripted or menu-based NPC dialogue (`16 CFR § 312.7`).**

### Plain-Sentence COPPA Rules with FTC Primary Pages Cited (All Read 2026-11-12)

1. **Why a Free Game Is Covered (`16 CFR § 312.2` at `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/section-312.2`, read 2026-11-12; FTC Six-Step Plan Step 1 at `https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-six-step-compliance-plan-your-business`, read 2026-11-12):**
   - COPPA applies to any commercial website or online service (including a free game operated by an LLC that has an in-game shop or commercial business model) that is either directed to children under 13 (including a "mixed audience" child-directed segment) or is a general-audience service that has "actual knowledge" that a specific player is under 13 (for example, from an age screen or from the player telling an NPC their age in chat).
2. **Why Free-Text Chat, AI Memory, Persistent IDs, and Voice Audio Trigger COPPA (`16 CFR § 312.2` at `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/section-312.2`, read 2026-11-12; FTC COPPA FAQs at `https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions`, read 2026-11-12):**
   - **Free-text chat and stored AI memory:** Under `16 CFR § 312.2`, "collects or collection" includes enabling a child to enter personal information in open text fields or chat unless the operator takes reasonable measures to delete all or virtually all personal information from the child's postings before they are stored or transmitted. Storing raw chat logs or building a persistent AI memory graph from what a child types collects personal information.
   - **Persistent identifiers:** Account IDs, cookies, IP addresses, or device serial numbers (`16 CFR § 312.2`) are personal information unless used strictly for "support for the internal operations of the website or online service" (such as maintaining basic game state, authenticating the user, or protecting site security, and never for behavioral profiling or unconsented AI memory profiling).
   - **Voice and audio recordings:** Under `16 CFR § 312.2`, an audio file containing a child's voice is personal information. Under the FTC's audio enforcement policy (explained in `https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions`, read 2026-11-12), an operator may only use a child's voice without prior parental consent if the voice input is used **solely as a replacement for written words** (such as a brief voice command), is held only long enough to transcribe the words, is **immediately deleted**, and is disclosed in the privacy policy.
3. **Neutral Age Screening (`https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions`, Section D, read 2026-11-12):**
   - A mixed-audience or all-ages game may screen players' ages at sign-up, but the age gate must be **neutral** (for example, asking for birth month and year or age without warning "You must be 13+ to use AI chat") and must set a session/device flag so an under-13 child cannot simply click the Back button and enter an older age.
4. **Clear Children's Privacy Policy and Direct Notice to Parents (`16 CFR § 312.4` at `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/section-312.4`, read 2026-11-12; FTC Six-Step Plan Steps 2 and 3 at `https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-six-step-compliance-plan-your-business`, read 2026-11-12):**
   - The game must post a clear, prominent link to its Children's Privacy Policy on the home page and at every point where personal information is collected.
   - Before collecting chat or AI memory from an under-13 child, the game must send a **Direct Notice** to the parent stating what information we want to collect (free-text chat and AI memory), how it will be used (processed by our server and Anthropic's commercial API under a zero-training data processing agreement), that the parent's consent is required, and how the parent can give consent.
5. **Verifiable Parental Consent (VPC) Before Storing Chat or Memory (`16 CFR § 312.5` at `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/section-312.5`, read 2026-11-12; FTC Six-Step Plan Step 4 at `https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-six-step-compliance-plan-your-business`, read 2026-11-12):**
   - Operators must *"Provide direct notice to parents and obtain verifiable parental consent, with limited exceptions, before collecting personal information online from children"* (`https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions`, read 2026-11-12).
   - If the child's chat and memory are used solely internally to power private NPC dialogue (never shared publicly with other players in chat rooms and never used by a third party for its own purposes, since Anthropic's Commercial Terms Section B prohibits training on Customer Content), `16 CFR § 312.5(b)(2)` permits the **"Email Plus"** VPC method (an email from the parent plus a delayed follow-up confirmation email, letter, or phone call notifying the parent that they can revoke consent at any time).
   - If the child's chat or information can be disclosed to other players (such as public multiplayer chat) or third parties, full heightened VPC is required under `16 CFR § 312.5(b)(2)` (such as a credit-card transaction, signed consent form, government ID check, or knowledge-based authentication).
6. **Never Block Gameplay for Refusing Extra Data Collection (`16 CFR § 312.7` at `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/section-312.7`, read 2026-11-12):**
   - Under `16 CFR § 312.7`, an operator is prohibited from conditioning a child's participation in a game on the child's disclosing more personal information than is reasonably necessary to participate in that activity. Therefore, any under-13 player whose parent has not granted VPC must still be able to play DREAM Online in full using safe **pre-scripted / menu-choice NPC dialogue** with zero stored free-text chat and zero personal AI memory.
7. **Parental Rights to Review, Delete, and Revoke (`16 CFR § 312.6` at `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/section-312.6`, read 2026-11-12; FTC Six-Step Plan Step 5):**
   - At any time, a parent must be able to review their child's stored chat and AI memory, require us to delete all stored chat and memory from our servers and graphs, and refuse any further collection or use of the child's personal information.
8. **Security, Data Minimization, and Retention Limits (`16 CFR §§ 312.8, 312.10` at `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/section-312.10`, read 2026-11-12; FTC Six-Step Plan Step 6):**
   - Operators must *"retain personal information collected online from a child for only as long as is necessary to fulfill the purpose for which it was collected and delete the information using reasonable measures to protect against its unauthorized access or use"* (`https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions`, read 2026-11-12), and must ensure that any third-party service provider (such as Anthropic) is contractually bound to maintain the confidentiality, security, and integrity of that data (`16 CFR § 312.8`).
